    01Built from your brief

    The Excel brief is the catalogue, one row a product. Each product's images are found in your folders by the code in the file or folder name, or by the product's name, a near match marked Check for you to confirm, and laid out in its cell; its copy is written from the brief's columns, in your paragraph styles. Product and row stay tied: an edit on either side, a new price in the brief or a renamed product on the page, is listed in Changes, to take whichever way you choose.

    02Pages on a live grid or no grid

    Every page is built on a grid: products across and down, and the gap between them, set in Setup ▸ Pages. Change it and Apply changes builds the pages again in one undo step, the products flowing on in the brief's order. Cells can be joined, across and down: here the Party pack takes the width of the page, Sparkling water two cells across and two down, and Lemon soda two across, and the products round them flow on. On Auto a short page gets a smaller grid, and a page break starts a new page wherever a column of the brief changes. Set a page to Free and it is yours to arrange: each product is placed once and never moved again, and its images are still laid out for its cell, wherever you put it and whatever its size.

    03Products managed by Assemblr layout engine

    Each product is a cell: an image area over its copy. In the image area Assemblr's layout engine arranges the images by what you can see in them, in Line-up, Grid, Mosaic, Pins or Puzzle, and Copy fitting gives the copy the room it needs and the image area the rest. Badges and prices go on its corners, each price written into your own price design. Drag an image into a cell and it joins the product, arranged with the rest; copy you edit on the page is kept until you choose to overwrite it. More on Assemblr ›

    04Inside your margins, with inserts

    The grid fills each page inside its margins, inside and outside swapped on facing pages, on the parent pages you choose. Anything that is not a product goes in as an insert: an InDesign file, a PDF, any image InDesign places, or a table filled from its own sheet and styled by a design you draw. It takes a full page, with or without bleed, half or a quarter of one, or any block of cells; it keeps its place in the product order or is pinned to a page, and the products flow round it. The panel tells you what size to make the file.

    05The product list

    Every product in the brief, in page order. Drag a product to move it: the pages are built again in the new order, and Changes lists how it now differs from the spreadsheet. Or work inside a product: drag its images to change their order, drag one in from another product or from the files no product matched, take one out with × or add one with +. ¶ starts a new page before a product, and + adds an insert after it.

Folders

Where the catalogue comes from: the brief, the folders of images, and how each file finds its product.

  1. 1

    Brief

    The Excel workbook the catalogue is built from, one row per product. Change… picks another; anything it cannot use is listed as a warning.

  2. 2

    Image folders

    The folders your packshots live in, and how many files each holds. A folder that has gone missing says so.

  3. 3

    Analyse links

    Reads the brief and the folders again and matches the files to products. Nothing on the pages changes until you apply it in Changes.

  4. 4

    Place images

    Puts every product's images on its page and re-fits them all, in steps you can stop; one undo step. Update reads changed files again and re-fits their products, leaving locked or turned images as they are.

  5. 5

    Matching

    The ways a file can find its product: the product code in the file name, in the folder name, or the product's name. Untick a way to stop using it, and drag to choose which one wins when two disagree: the higher one decides.

  6. 6

    Low confidence

    Files matched only by a name that looks alike are placed but marked Check, so you confirm each one on its product.

  7. 7

    Prefer file types

    When one image is in the folders as both a PSD and a TIF, keep only this type.

Pages

How the pages are built: the grid each page gets, how many pages, their parent pages and where a new page starts.

  1. 1

    Grid: Auto or Consistent

    Auto: a page with fewer products gets a smaller grid, its cells the shape of a full page's. Consistent: every page gets the grid you set, and a short page keeps its empty slots.

  2. 2

    Across, Down and Gap

    Products across and down a page, and the space between them in points. Left on auto, they follow from the number of products.

  3. 3

    Copy height

    The share of each cell the copy takes, under its image area.

  4. 4

    Page count

    How many pages the catalogue runs to (auto gives it as many as it needs), and the page it starts on.

  5. 5

    Masters

    The parent pages the left-hand and right-hand pages are built on.

  6. 6

    Page breaks

    A brief column, a category say: a new page starts wherever its value changes.

  7. 7

    Apply changes

    The settings are a draft until Apply changes builds the pages with them, in one undo step. Discard throws them away; nothing is written.

Copy

What each product's copy says, from which columns of the brief, in which paragraph styles.

  1. 1

    Copy fitting

    Off: every cell splits at the same height, whatever its text. Per product: each cell's copy takes what it needs and the image area gets the rest. Match the row: every cell in a row takes the row's largest copy height, so the captions all start level, as most printed catalogues do.

  2. 2

    Fields

    A row for each column of the brief: tick it to show it in the copy, and use the arrows to change the order.

  3. 3

    How each field starts

    On a new paragraph, after a line break, a space or a tab, in the paragraph style you choose.

  4. 4

    Paragraph styles

    Automatic finds the document's style with the field's name. With none, Create style makes it; a style found by a near name waits for you to confirm it.

  5. 5

    Leave out empty fields

    A product with nothing in a column gets no empty line for it.

  6. 6

    Write copy

    Writes every product's copy from the brief. Copy edited in InDesign is counted and kept until you choose to overwrite it.

Tables

Price lists and spec tables, each filled from its own sheet and styled by a design you draw in InDesign.

  1. 1

    Table Setup

    A parent page that holds your table designs. Show turns the window to it, and the panel warns you if it is applied to a page that prints.

  2. 2

    Designs

    A table drawn in InDesign, a header row and one sample row: its columns, styles and strokes are what every table that follows it gets. Use selection as design adds the one you have selected.

  3. 3

    Tables

    Each table comes from a sheet and follows a design. The panel lists the cells that change and the rows added or removed, and keeps any cell you edited by hand.

  4. 4

    Problems, said plainly

    A design with no sample row, or a table taller than its frame, is named with what to do about it.

  5. 5

    Refresh tables

    Reads every table's sheet and design and lists what would change. Nothing changes until you apply it, table by table or all at once.

Layout

How the selected product's images are arranged, by what you can see in them rather than their frames: Assemblr's layout engine, inside the catalogue.

  1. 1

    The drawing

    The product drawn over the panel: drag the line's end to turn the row and set its size, and pick a dot to choose the edge or corner the product holds to.

  2. 2

    Arrangements

    Line-up, Grid, Mosaic, Pins and Puzzle: five ways to lay out a product's images, each fitted to the visible subject of every image.

  3. 3

    The product

    The product the selection is in, its page, and its images. Select them all picks every frame it is made of.

  4. 4

    Settings

    Align images, Angle, Size, Scale, Bleed, Perspective and a clipping path from each image's transparency, as each arrangement has them.

  5. 5

    Apply to page, Apply to all

    Give every product on this page, or in the catalogue, these settings.

  6. 6

    Re-fit and Reset

    Re-fit lays the images out again. Reset puts the product back to the arrangement's automatic settings, hand rotation cleared and locks released, in one undo step; Reset page and Reset all do the same wider.

  7. 7

    Images

    The product's images in order. Drag to reorder, click one to leave it out, + to add one; Unassigned files lists the files that matched no product.

Overlays

Badges and prices on the corners of a product, from a folder of artwork or a column of the brief.

  1. 1

    Where it applies

    One product, wherever it goes; a range of pages; or the whole document. A product follows the document until you give it its own.

  2. 2

    Corner and Stack

    Pick a corner, on the drawing or here; its overlays stack across or down from it. Click another corner to move them there, or swap them with what is there.

  3. 3

    Sources

    What goes on the corner: badges from the overlays folder, or + Column, a column of the brief whose cells name what goes there.

  4. 4

    Size and spacing

    Gap and Inset X and Y in points, or scaled with the product by Resize scale; each overlay fitted into a Max width by Max height, in points or as a share of the product.

  5. 5

    Overlays folder

    One folder of artwork: badges, and price devices, InDesign files the plugin writes each product's price into.

  6. 6

    Price sets

    Another folder of price devices, another look for the prices. Each corner with a price chooses the set it is drawn from.

Catalogue

Every product in the brief, in page order, each with where it stands.

  1. 1

    No image

    Shows only the products still waiting for an image.

  2. 2

    Order

    Drag a product to reorder it: the pages are rebuilt in the new order, and Changes lists the difference from the spreadsheet.

  3. 3

    Where each product stands

    Its page, its images placed or added, copy overset, an image changed on disk (Update), on the pasteboard, or edited in InDesign.

  4. 4

    Check

    A file matched only by a name that looks alike waits on its product until you say Looks right or Not this product.

  5. 5

    Breaks and inserts

    ¶ starts a new page before a product; + adds an insert after it, an advert or a table, from an INDD, a PDF or an image.

Grid

The selected page's grid, and how many slots each product on it takes.

  1. 1

    Managed or Free

    Managed: the plugin keeps this page on its grid. Free: you arrange the page, and the plugin leaves it alone.

  2. 2

    The page

    The page drawn as its grid, each product in its slots. Set… opens this page's own grid and spacing in Setup ▸ Pages.

  3. 3

    Size

    Give a product more than one slot, a 2 × 2 hero say, and the products round it flow on. One slot puts it back.

  1. 1

    Check

    Reads the spreadsheet again and compares it with the pages; the tables' sheets are read too.

    Brings every edit made in the brief into the document.

  3. 3

03Help

Quick help

What the settings alone do not tell you.

Price devices

  • Make your price devices

    Each price device is an InDesign file of its own, kept in the overlays folder or in a folder of its own as a price set. Automated Assemblr writes each product's price from the brief into the device that fits it.

    PricesSet 1

    • x-xx.indd$X.XX
    • xx-xx.indd$XX.XX
    • xx-c.inddXXc

    Prices saleSet 2

    • x-xx.indd$X.XX
    • xx-xx.indd$XX.XX
    • xx-c.inddXXc
    One file, one device
    Save each device as an .indd, .idml or .idms file. Build it as you like, on as many layers as it needs: a panel or a shape behind, images, and the text frames for the price. It is placed as one, on the product's corner, and follows the product.
    Text mapping
    Type X where the digits go: $X.XX for dollars and cents, XXc for cents alone. Each product's price is written into those runs only, so the $, the point, the c and every paragraph and character style round them stay as you set them; superscript cents stay superscript. No tagging needed.
    More fields from the brief
    Tag a text frame, or a run of its text, with the name of a column of your brief, Unit say, in InDesign's Tags panel: each product's value in that column is written there. A tag that names no column is left as you typed it.
    A folder a set, a file a kind
    One folder holds a device for each kind of price: x-xx, xx-xx and xx-c side by side, $5.50 takes the first, $12.50 the second and 95c the third. Name a file for its shape to settle a device its text leaves open. Keep another look, a sale price say, in a folder of its own: Add price set… makes it Set 2, and each corner with a price chooses its set, for the document, a range of pages or one product. Two devices of one shape and size in one folder clash, and neither is used.
